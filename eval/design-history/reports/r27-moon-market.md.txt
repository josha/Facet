## What was built

Moon Market is a fitting room for low-gravity streetwear. Nine original native 3D accessories span Caps, Visors and Packs. The catalog and avatar share the same geometry. Players inspect and try on products, buy with demo credits, add or remove owned accessories, review and apply an outfit, or discard a draft while retaining purchases. A Turn action exposes rear equipment. This is a complete local demonstration, not a production commerce system.

The brief prescribed outcomes, supported inputs and art constraints. It did not prescribe a radial menu, popup, catalog control, screen layout or theme. The author chose category buttons, merchandise tiles, an adjacent live preview, item actions, a stable outfit bar and confirmation dialogs. No original UI bitmap assets or uploads were needed for the chosen graphic/3D direction.

## Quality judgment and remaining limits

The avatar is a convincing original toy-like subject, with a face, controlled highlights, costume seams, badge and pedestal. The same accessory silhouettes appear on the merchandise tickets and avatar. The orbital booth, slanted title, plum/lilac tickets and lime action treatment create an identity beyond a palette swap. The result is pleasant and coherent, and the controls support the actual inspection/purchase/outfit decisions.

The first representative screen was not finished: the review loop materially improved both its control treatment and device fit. The final confirmation panels are still quieter and more generic than the shop; their styling is the weakest part of the visual direction. Small product models in short landscape and secondary console text leave less visual margin than the desktop subject. Physical TV/couch readability is unverified. This is good game-prototype craft, not evidence of universal production polish.

Desktop keyboard navigation remains **unverified**. Both Studio MCP and direct engine VirtualInput delivered zero Keyboard events for ordinary letters, arrows, Tab and Return, including after simulation stopped and GamepadEnabled became false. The same host delivered pointer events. No keyboard success is inferred from assigned focus or controller navigation.

Studio also logged an asset-type mismatch for asset 203785492 and occasional invalid-selection warnings. The game source does not reference that asset. No missing authored UI asset or failing return focus was observed in the tested routes; these warnings remain disclosed rather than called resolved. There are no remaining blocking pointer, Touch or Gamepad1 failures observed in the final tested routes.

## What this implies for guidance

Keep the composition-first direction and the separate fresh-review workflow. This case transferred to an unseen UI-heavy task and produced an original subject, coherent merchandise treatment and appropriate controls without forcing a menu type. Three reviews were useful and caught real defects; they were not proof of complete device coverage.

Do not claim a reliable first-delivery finish yet. A named phone preset and a clean default sweep missed a smaller safe viewport. The parent found an ordinary minimum-height budgeting failure after the author had declared delivery. The existing compact-composition guidance already explains reserving the task body after header/footer allocation and explicitly warns that a minimum body can exceed the available height. Adding more visual prose would repeat an existing rule.

The strongest next improvement is verification coverage: declare the minimum supported safe viewport and exercise it early, including the whole painted bounds and reachability of the decision actions. Keep a notched-phone case too. Improve the default device/evidence coverage before adding another style checklist. This evaluation leaves the frozen design guidance unchanged; no new guidance revision or publication is claimed from this trial.

One fresh avatar trial is directional evidence, not a general success-rate estimate.
